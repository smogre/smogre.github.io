Research

My research combines biophysical theory, multi-scale simulations, and machine learning with high-throughput 3D and 4D bioimaging to study how biological systems organize and build themselves across scales: from molecular motors and cytoskeletal filaments to 3D organelle packing (cellPACK), single-cell morphodynamics, and multicellular tissues.

Generative Modeling of Intracellular Organization

I develop geometry-aware generative modeling frameworks using cellPACK to simulate, test, and quantify 3D spatial intracellular organization in hiPSCs. By conditioning rule-based 3D packings on observed cell and nuclear geometries and introducing an available-volume-normalized occupancy metric, our work disentangles geometric confounding from biological spatial enrichment across hundreds of single-cell images, accompanied by cellPACK Studio for interactive browser-based 3D simulation. We also used cellPACK to generate synthetic 3D organelle distributions with known spatial rules to benchmark rotation-invariant point-cloud representation learning models of multi-piece intracellular structures.

Compositional & Multi-Scale Biophysical Modeling

In this collaborative work, we compared molecular- and mesoscopic-scale simulation engines (including ReaDDy particle-based reaction-diffusion and Cytosim filament mechanics) to establish principles for compositional biophysical modeling. By benchmarking actin filament compression and buckling across scales, we quantified trade-offs between computational cost and structural fidelity (such as capturing helical supertwist) to guide multi-algorithmic cellular simulations. We are now working on extending this framework to compose models across scales from molecular dynamics to multicellular systems.

Colony Context & Nuclear Morphogenesis

As part of a large-scale 4D (3D + time) live-cell imaging study, I contributed quantitative trajectory analysis and modeling to investigate how local colony crowding and size-dependent compensation mechanisms regulate nuclear growth and size homeostasis across human iPSC populations.

Endothelial Cell Morphodynamics Under Shear Stress

In this collaborative study, we characterized a shear stress-dependent bifurcation in hiPSC-derived endothelial cell states identified via unsupervised machine learning on time-lapse microscopy.

Axonal Autophagy & Organelle Maturation Dynamics

I integrated quantitative trajectory analysis of dual-color live-cell microscopy with stochastic reaction-transport modeling to uncover how autophagosomes mature along neuronal axons. Our work showed that retrograde autophagosomes undergo remarkably few lysosome fusion events and exhibit slow, two-step luminal acidification and degradative maturation, identifying key physical and kinetic bottlenecks in neuronal clearance.

Organelle Hitchhiking & Cytoskeletal Network Optimization

I built 3D Brownian dynamics and Monte Carlo simulations (in C++/CUDA and FORTRAN) to investigate how organelles initiate directed transport and capture molecular motors. We modeled the mechanical and geometric determinants of linker-mediated organelle hitchhiking on carrier vesicles, and identified optimal microtubule length distributions and minus-end polarity arrangements that maximize rapid dynein-driven cargo capture in elongated cells.

Multimodal Transport & Diffusive Search in Constrained Geometries

I developed analytical master-equation and propagator frameworks to characterize how intracellular cargo navigates narrow tubular cells and interconnected networks. These models quantify how alternating motor-driven runs, passive diffusion, and reversible tethering govern organelle dispersion, mixing, and first-passage search times across cellular scales.

Hydrodynamics & Domain Diffusion in Lipid Bilayer Membranes

We derived analytical expressions for the lateral diffusion coefficients of circular domains in lipid bilayer membranes, accounting for interleaflet friction, leaflet viscosity contrasts, and hydrodynamic coupling to the surrounding solvent and substrate.
